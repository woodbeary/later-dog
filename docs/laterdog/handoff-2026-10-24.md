# Handoff 2026-10-24: homes wake faster, and free-model dogs answer while their home sleeps

Follows [handoff-2026-10-23.md](handoff-2026-10-23.md).

## What changed

| Change | Where |
| --- | --- |
| A Cloud home answers without waiting on slow engine model lists. On a cold start, reading Cursor's list took 16.5 s and Codex's 9 s, and the server listened only after both. Each engine now waits at most 300 ms, starts with its built-in list and takes the full one when it arrives | `server/laterdog/wait-at-most.ts`, the agent, Claude and Codex drivers |
| A send held for a sleeping home names its thread in the receipt, so the desktop shows it as waiting instead of "Sending…" forever, and saved chat lists include held sends, so a refresh while the home sleeps keeps them | `deploy/laterdog/home/src/desk.ts` |
| Free-model dogs answer at once while their home sleeps. Before sleeping, the home's Worker reads each free-model dog's notes from the home (its last prompt and recent words, on a signed request). A plain message from the owner's own device to such a dog is then answered by the included model straight away and streamed to the owner's open apps while the home wakes. Anything that needs the computer, files or tools is left for the home. Once the home is up, the Worker hands over each answered message with a reply signed for that send and those exact words, and the home records it as the dog's answer without running the dog again | `deploy/laterdog/home/src/desk-answer.ts`, `desk-stream.ts`, `host.ts`; `server/laterdog/desk-brain.ts`, `desk-exchange.ts` |

## Limits

- Only the included free models (Kimi K2.6 and the free Grok). Dogs on API keys or on Claude and ChatGPT sign-ins still wait for the home.
- Only plain text from the owner's own devices to a dog's main chat; attachments, replies to a line, rooms and guests go to the home.
- A dog needs one free-model turn before its home can answer for it, and the Worker reads the notes when the home goes to sleep and at most every five minutes while it is in use.
- Answers written while asleep have no tools, memory writes or computer; the dog is told so and calls for its computer instead.
- The desktop shows such an answer when it is finished; the phone streams it.
- An answer written while asleep sends no push notification; the person who sent it has the app open.

## Later on 2026-10-24: the owner's home

The owner said the app was unusable. Read from his home (read-only probes with the dev-tools session, and its logs by
container id in Workers Observability):

- **Replies are slow, and that is mostly the engines.** From his chats: "reply with pong" on Claude took 6 to 24 s,
  "hi" on Codex 32 to 35 s. Claude's CLI stays running for 10 minutes after a message
  (`server/drivers/session-idle.ts`), so the first message after a quiet spell pays its start. Each home now logs one line
  per reply with no words from the chat (`server/laterdog/turn-timing.ts`): engine, model, and how long after the message
  the reply started, said its first words and finished. Read those before deciding anything about machine size.
- **The accounts list took 11.8 s after a quiet spell and 2.1 s otherwise**, since every account's check runs its CLI.
  `server/laterdog/snapshot-cache.ts` now keeps answers for a day and saves them with the home; an account whose check
  takes over half a second is answered at once from its last check and checked again at most every 30 s; answers saved
  before a restart are shown at once and checked again straight away.
- **Engine model lists on a Cloud home are read 30 s after it starts** (`readEngineModels`), so those CLIs do not compete
  with the first requests after a wake. The desktop still reads them at once, waiting at most 300 ms.
- **The rollout left his always-on home down for almost nine minutes.** The 17:16 rollout stopped it and nothing started
  it until the ten-minute check at 17:24:48. Always-on homes now ask to start five seconds after their computer stops, and
  check every minute (Worker `11e25de4`, live).
- **A rollout replaces running containers**, including ones that started during it on the earlier version: on a test
  home a container that came up at 18:05:23 on `ca02360f63a2` got SIGTERM at 18:05:35 and came back on `459ae7c93334`.
  Requests in between answer 502 or "the container is not running". Judge a change only after the rollout settles.
- **More CPU does not shorten a wake.** Test homes on a custom 1 vCPU type (memory unchanged): the server was listening
  about 5 s after its files were restored (about 14 s at 1/2 vCPU on the same home), but a whole cold wake still took
  about 10 s, as at 1/2 vCPU, and the first start on the new type took 2 minutes. Test homes are back on `standard-1`.
  Cloudflare bills CPU by active use and memory by size, so more vCPU costs little; whether it helps replies is for the
  reply timing to show.
